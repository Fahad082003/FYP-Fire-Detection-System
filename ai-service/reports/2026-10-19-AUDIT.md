# FireVision AI (Sentinel): AI and end-to-end evaluation audit, 2026-10-19

Scope: every AI capability, the path from model output to dashboard, failure handling, load, and dependency security.

Rule followed: **measured or not claimed.**
- Every number below comes from a command run on 2026-10-19, unless it is marked with an earlier date.
- Machine: Xeon W3520, 4 threads, no AVX, no GPU, Windows 11, ONNX Runtime CPU.
- All live tests used throwaway databases (`SentinelDb_LoadTest` and others) that were dropped afterwards, plus throwaway keys.
- `.env` was **not** loaded, so no SMS, email or push was sent, and no hardware was connected.
- The user's `SentinelDb` was not touched.

## A. Executive summary

**Readiness**
- **Ready for a controlled demo.**
- **Limited pilot only** with human oversight, and only after evaluation on site footage.
- **Not production-ready, and not a safety system.** Keep the certified fire panel and smoke detectors.

**What was verified against real model output**
- Person detection: Penn-Fudan, 170 images, 423 people.
  - AP50 = **0.966**; AP50:95 = 0.775.
  - Recall 0.993, precision 0.707 at the configured threshold 0.45.
- Fire and smoke detection: official D-Fire test split, 4,306 images.
  - Fire: AP50 = **0.685**, AP50:95 = 0.342.
  - Smoke: AP50 = **0.803**, AP50:95 = 0.474.
  - Image-level, at 0.40: fire recall **0.932** [95% CI 0.916–0.945] with false-positive rate 0.006; smoke recall **0.923** [0.910–0.933] with false-positive rate 0.028.
- Live path, all with real models on a test stack:
  - test camera → AI service → API → SignalR → incident → danger zone → evacuation route changes → risk heatmap.
  - The automatic test `tools/live_test.py` passed 8/8 in three runs on 2026-10-16.

**Critical defects:** none found.

**Defects fixed in this audit:**
- D-01: two vulnerable transitive .NET packages (High, denial of service).
- Same-day remediation (section H): D-02 (deleted camera frames), D-04 (rate limit), D-06 (NaN boxes), D-10 (severity double counting, newly found) and D-11 (stopped state).

**Major risks (open)**
- **D-03 capacity:** inference runs one frame at a time. With 3 cameras, each camera gets **0.62 frames/s**, so the 3-frame fire confirmation takes about 5 s per camera.
- **D-09 severity:** fire box recall is only 0.65, so the area-based severity is under-estimated.
- **Untested conditions:** site CCTV, IR or night cameras, fog, steam, crowds, and top-down angles.

**Next actions**
1. Evaluate on labelled footage from the target site, including empty scenes.
2. Decide the per-camera frame rate or hardware for the number of cameras needed.
3. Add a rate limit to `/api/ai/analyze`.

## C. Prediction evaluation (real models)

### How the metrics were computed
- Tool and command:
  `python tools/evaluate.py --data <set> --classes … --sweep … --out reports/2026-10-19-…`
- Matching rules:
  - Greedy, highest confidence first. A prediction is a true positive (TP) if IoU ≥ 0.5 with an unmatched ground-truth box of the same class.
  - Duplicate predictions are false positives (FP). Unmatched ground-truth boxes are false negatives (FN).
- **AP (average precision):** area under the precision–recall curve, computed COCO-style with 101-point interpolation.
  - Uses every prediction with confidence ≥ 0.01, for all object sizes, with no crowd regions.
  - **AP50** is AP at IoU 0.50. **AP50:95** is the mean AP over IoU 0.50, 0.55, …, 0.95, which rewards tight boxes.
  - The AP code is unit-tested against a hand-computed example (`AveragePrecisionTests`).
- **Inference floor:** inference ran once at a confidence floor of 0.01, then results were filtered per threshold.
  - NMS is greedy with no top-k limit, so the boxes kept at each threshold are identical to running at that threshold directly.
  - Proof: the person numbers at 0.45 reproduce the 2026-10-13 report exactly (TP 420, FP 174, FN 3).
- **Image-level metrics:** an image counts as detected if it has at least one box of the class. The image-level false-positive rate (FPR) is computed over images with no box of that class.
- **CI:** 95% Wilson confidence interval.
- Per-image ground truth, predictions, confidences and latency are saved in `predictions.jsonl` next to each `report.json`.

### Threshold bias
- The configured thresholds (person 0.45, fire/smoke 0.40) were fixed **before** these test-set runs:
  - 0.45 is the YOLOX default used since the first report.
  - 0.40 was the manifest default before the GPU model existed.
- The sweep tables below come from the **test** split, so they must not be used to pick a new threshold and then be reported again as unbiased.
- Choosing a new threshold needs a separate validation split.

## I. Synthetic stress test: darkness and fog (2026-10-19)

**SYNTHETIC: these are not real night, IR or fog images.**
- `tools/degrade.py` darkens images (brightness ×0.25 plus sensor-like noise) and/or adds uniform haze (transmission 0.45, light-grey airlight). Labels are unchanged. The script is unit-tested.
- Fire/smoke: a fixed random sample of 1,000 D-Fire test images (seed 0): 271 with fire, 467 with smoke, 729 without fire. The clean copy of the same sample is the baseline.
- Person: all 170 Penn-Fudan images.
- Configured thresholds: person 0.45, fire/smoke 0.40. Results are in [2026-10-19-stress-synthetic/](2026-10-19-stress-synthetic/).
- The clean baseline was re-saved as JPEG quality 95, so it differs very slightly from section C. Person AP50 is 0.965 here vs 0.966.

### Person (box level, IoU 0.5)

| Condition | Recall [95% CI] | Precision | AP50 | AP50:95 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clean | 0.993 [0.979–0.998] | 0.707 | 0.965 | 0.772 |
| Dark | 0.983 [0.966–0.992] | 0.786 | 0.972 | 0.737 |
| Fog | 0.991 [0.976–0.996] | 0.730 | 0.968 | 0.758 |
| Dark + fog | **0.858** [0.822–0.888] | 0.860 | 0.911 | 0.572 |

### Fire and smoke (image level, at 0.40)

| Condition | Fire recall [95% CI] | Fire FPR | Smoke recall [95% CI] | Smoke FPR | Fire / smoke AP50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clean | 0.941 [0.906–0.963] | 0.005 | 0.927 [0.900–0.947] | 0.034 | 0.695 / 0.791 |
| Fog | **0.727** [0.671–0.777] | 0.003 | **0.687** [0.644–0.728] | 0.043 | 0.468 / 0.595 |
| Dark | 0.022 [0.010–0.047] | 0.000 | 0.283 [0.244–0.325] | 0.023 | 0.093 / 0.240 |
| Dark + fog | 0.000 [0.000–0.014] | 0.000 | 0.060 [0.042–0.085] | 0.015 | 0.010 / 0.025 |

### How to read this
- **People** stay well detected in synthetic darkness and in fog. Only dark *and* fog together costs about 14% recall.
- **Fog** is a real weakness: fire recall drops from 0.94 to 0.73 and smoke recall from 0.93 to 0.69. Haze hides thin smoke and washes out flame colour.
- **"Dark" is not a fair model of a fire at night.** The transform also dims the flames, but real flames are light sources and stay bright.
  - On the **real** low-light D-Fire test images (mean grey < 60, from section C's predictions), fire recall is **0.977** (515/527, CI 0.961–0.987) and smoke recall 0.932 (372/399).
  - The synthetic dark result therefore stands for an **under-exposed camera** (flames rendered dim), not for night in general.
  - Practical consequence: keep camera exposure automatic and never let flames be rendered dark.
- **False alarms do not rise** in darkness. In fog the smoke FPR rises slightly (0.034 → 0.043).

### Decision
- No change to thresholds or models.
- Next evidence needed: **real** fog, steam and IR footage (labelled site clips, see the "Raasta 2" plan).
- If fog matters for the site, fine-tuning with fog augmentation is the first candidate. It must be re-evaluated on the same protocol before any claim.

### Experiment: contrast enhancement before the AI (2026-10-19)

`tools/enhance.py` applies an enhancement to each image before detection. Same 1,000 D-Fire and 170 Penn-Fudan images; models and thresholds unchanged. Two methods:
- **CLAHE:** local contrast on lightness only.
- **Stretch:** per-channel 1st–99th percentile stretched to the full range.

- **Not enabled** in the live service, and not added as a setting yet. The only evidence for stretch comes from fog that favours it.

## L. Banner delay and camera Status (2026-10-21)

### Issue 1: camera-offline banner up to ~30 s late
- **Root cause:** only the dashboard's poll timer.
  - `shell.component.ts` polled `/api/ai/status` every **30 s**.
  - The API proxies the AI service's `/health` with a 3 s timeout. That handler takes no lock and returns stored state, so it is cheap.
- **Measured live:** after a camera stream was cut, `/api/ai/status` showed the camera as `error` after **0.6 s**. The delay was therefore almost all polling.
- **Change:** poll every **10 s** (`AI_POLL_MS`). This is 6 light requests per minute per open dashboard, negligible next to 2 analysed frames per second per camera.
- **Expected banner delay:** at most about 0.6 s + 10 s ≈ **11 s**, down from about 31 s. The "Camera lost" *incident* is unchanged: 10 s of silence plus the 2 s watchdog check.
- **Banner rules:**
  - Open incidents always win.
  - With no incidents, the reasons are checked in this order: link down → AI status unknown (status call failed) → AI offline → model missing → any camera `error` or `reconnecting` → otherwise "ALL SYSTEMS NOMINAL".
  - A deleted camera (`stopped`) is not a failure.
  - A camera the AI has not picked up yet (≤ 60 s sync) does not show.
  - A reconnect never clears an open fire or camera-lost incident.

### Issue 2 (D-16): `CameraFeed.Status` never changed
- **Root cause:** nothing wrote the field after it was created.
  - It is `Online` by default, it is returned by the API, and the dashboard does not display it.
  - Intended meaning, from the enum and the DTO: Online / Degraded / Offline.
- **Change**, using the existing watchdog rule; no schema change, and Degraded remains unused:
  - Camera lost (no frames for `Alerts:CameraLostSeconds` = 10 s) → `Offline`, saved together with the camera-lost incident (`AlertEngine.CameraLostAsync`).
  - Fresh frames → `Online` (`CameraWatchdog`).
    - This is written once per process and after every loss, so a status left `Offline` before an API restart is corrected by the first frames.
    - The in-memory flag is set only after the write succeeds, so a failed write is retried.
  - Only real frames change the status. AI health errors never do.
  - Unknown ids update 0 rows. Deleted cameras have no row.
- **Measured live:** `Offline` 12.7 s after the stream was cut; `Online` again 5.3 s after it came back; the camera-lost incident stayed open.
- **Limit:** a camera that dies *during* an API restart keeps its last stored status, because it is never seen again (D-17). The banner's camera check covers that case.

## N. API restart with a dead camera, and the IL-9 banner gap (2026-10-21)

### Issue 1: API restart while a camera is already down (D-17)
- **Root cause.** `CameraWatchdog` only watched ids that had sent a frame since the API started.
  - A camera that died before a restart was never watched again: no camera-lost alarm, and `CameraFeed.Status` stayed at its last value.
- **Source of truth for "should be streaming".** Cameras in the database with a stream link. This is exactly the list the AI service reads (`api_cameras`).
- **Change: start-up reconciliation.** In `WatchStreamingCamerasAsync`, run once when the watchdog starts, each streaming camera is treated as seen at start-up minus the lost limit.   - A real frame already received wins (`TryAdd`).
  - Simulated feeds without a link, deleted cameras and `config.json`-only cameras are not watched.
  - The 60 s grace avoids false alarms while the AI service starts up, loads models and connects.
- **Live result** (camera killed, API restarted ~1 s later, camera kept down):
  - camera-lost alarm **61.7 s** after the API was up (before: never);
  - DB `Offline` **60.7 s** (before: stale `Online`);
  - banner "CAMERA OFFLINE" 3.7 s (via the AI's camera state).
- **Reconnect.** DB `Online` 34.7 s after the camera came back, and the camera-lost incident stayed open.
  - The slow recovery after a long outage comes from the AI worker's existing reconnect back-off (doubling to 30 s; `service.py`), not from this change. After a short outage it was 1.3 s (section M).

### Issue 2 (IL-9): green banner for up to ~11 s after the AI dies
- **Root cause.** The banner learnt about the AI only from the 10 s status poll, and an open incident hid any degraded reason.
- **Change** (`shell.component.ts`), with no extra requests and the poll still 10 s:
  1. **Frame staleness.** The dashboard already receives every AI frame over SignalR. A camera (still in the camera list) whose frames stopped **8–120 s** ago marks monitoring "AI DATA STALE".
     - 8 s is above the longest normal gap measured at 3–4 cameras (5.4 s), so it does not flicker.
     - Older silences are left to the AI poll and the camera-lost alarm.
  2. **Combined banner.** Open incidents stay first, and the reason is appended with the real count, e.g. "ELEVATED RISK · 1 OPEN · AI OFFLINE".
  3. **Stale replies ignored.** Each status poll is numbered; a slower older reply never overwrites a newer one.
- **Order of reasons:** live link down → AI status unknown → AI offline → model missing → camera offline (AI) → AI data stale.
- **Live result** (AI killed, real dashboard in Edge, 3 trials): first non-nominal banner after **8.2 s, 8.5 s and 3.1 s** ("AI DATA STALE" twice, "AI OFFLINE" once when a poll came first).
  - Worst case is about 9 s (8 s plus a 1 s tick), always before the ~11 s camera-lost alarm.
  - Before this change the banner stayed green until the alarm (section M, IL-9).
- **Regressions:**
  - Browser, fake clock: `IL-9: AI dies between polls…`. Frames at 1/s, stop, "AI DATA STALE" within 9 s, poll → "AI OFFLINE", incident → "ELEVATED RISK · 1 OPEN · AI OFFLINE", recovery → "ELEVATED RISK · 1 OPEN" with the incident still open. Passed 3/3 runs.
  - Unit: `shell.component.spec.ts`, a late older reply is ignored.
  - That ordering cannot be tested in Playwright, because its route handlers run one at a time.
  - Test-only notes:

### Remaining limits
- After an API restart a dead camera alarms only after the 60 s grace. Until then the banner shows "CAMERA OFFLINE" from the AI.
- After a long outage the AI may take up to its 30 s back-off to reconnect.
- "AI DATA STALE" relies on the browser's camera list, so a camera deleted in *another* browser can flag stale frames for up to 2 min until a reload.
- **Not production-ready, not safety-certified.**
